How we decide

How a product gets on this page.

One binary question at a time. A product does not pass on balance, and it does not fail on a hunch. It fails on a named rule, and we publish the rule.

There is no legal limit to fall back on.

No regulator sets a heavy-metal limit for protein powder, and nobody is required to test a batch before it reaches you. That absence is the reason this page exists.

A protein number is nitrogen multiplied by 6.25.

That is the lawful way to calculate it. Cheap non-protein ingredients raise the nitrogen and therefore raise the number on the panel. It is legal, it is common enough to have been litigated, and we reject products that do it.

Most people do not know their own number.

Roughly four in five adults are unsure how much protein they need. The research consensus for an exercising adult is 1.4 to 2.0 grams per kilogram per day, in doses of 20 to 40 grams carrying 0.7 to 3.0 grams of leucine. Every product below is measured against that.

We read labels. We do not test tubs.

Everything here comes from published panels, certificates and testing other people paid for. We have not commissioned a single assay, so we will never tell you these twelve are the cleanest. We will tell you exactly what we rejected and why.

19 read. 7 failed on an ingredient.

Not one of these was rejected for tasting bad or costing too much. Each broke a rule we had written down before we read it.

What this is, and what it is not.

  • We hold no stock and sell nothing. Every link goes to a merchant, and a commission on some of them never touches the ranking, which is finished before any price is attached.
  • Product records are authored from published formulations and manufacturer panels. Where a record was not re-verified against a live listing it is marked as reported, not confirmed.
  • Pack illustrations are drawn from each product's real container format and brand colours. They are drawings, not photographs, because the photographs available to us for this shelf turned out to show the wrong product in four cases.
  • Nineteen products is a reading list, not a survey of the market. We will keep reading, and this page will change when we do.
