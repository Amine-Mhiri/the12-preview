What we eliminated in pre-workout

12 of the 16 products we analysed never reached a score. Each one broke a rule that points at a regulator, a trial or a published re-evaluation — and each row below says which.

Some of these are excellent brands. Being on this page is not an accusation of bad faith; it means one ingredient in one formulation did not survive a doctrine that refuses to make exceptions.

Why they went

  • Sucralose12
  • Acesulfame potassium10
  • Allura Red AC8
  • Brilliant Blue FCF5
  • Caffeine above the type threshold, or total caffeine not stated4
  • Proprietary blend with a single total weight4
  • Active named with no individual amount4

The label we read

L-citrulline, beta-alanine, betaine anhydrous, L-tyrosine, taurine, alpha-GPC, natural and artificial flavors, citric acid, sucralose, acesulfame potassium, FD&C Red No. 40

Full analysis

  • Acesulfame potassium

    Safe by the regulator's assessment. We still exclude it, under WHO's 2023 guidance on non-sugar sweeteners and because it is the co-sweetener most often stacked with sucralose in this category.

    To be accurate about this one: EFSA completed a full re-evaluation in 2025 and raised the acceptable daily intake to 15 mg/kg of body weight per day, finding European exposure below that level in every population group. We still exclude it, but that is a values position rather than a toxicological finding, and we would rather say so than imply the regulators are on our side.

  • Sucralose

    A 2023 in vitro study raised questions about a manufacturing impurity of sucralose. The regulators have not moved. We have — and we exclude it, as we do the other synthetic high-intensity sweeteners, under WHO's 2023 guidance.

    This is the most frequent single cause of elimination in this vertical, roughly 52% of all sweetener rejections, so the caveat matters. The sucralose-6-acetate paper is an in vitro screening study, it is contested by industry and by several toxicologists, and no regulator has changed sucralose's status because of it. That is why you will see us put it as "a 2023 in vitro study reported…" and why you will not see us describe sucralose as genotoxic.

  • Allura Red AC

    One of the six colours that must carry the EU warning "may have an adverse effect on activity and attention in children". A dye that comes with a child-attention warning has no place in a family catalogue, and colour does nothing for you anyway.

    The EU response to the Southampton study was a mandatory warning label, not a ban. We rest the rejection on the warning label, which is a regulatory fact, not on a settled causal finding about any one child.

Full analysis

  • Acesulfame potassium

    Safe by the regulator's assessment. We still exclude it, under WHO's 2023 guidance on non-sugar sweeteners and because it is the co-sweetener most often stacked with sucralose in this category.

    To be accurate about this one: EFSA completed a full re-evaluation in 2025 and raised the acceptable daily intake to 15 mg/kg of body weight per day, finding European exposure below that level in every population group. We still exclude it, but that is a values position rather than a toxicological finding, and we would rather say so than imply the regulators are on our side.

  • Sucralose

    A 2023 in vitro study raised questions about a manufacturing impurity of sucralose. The regulators have not moved. We have — and we exclude it, as we do the other synthetic high-intensity sweeteners, under WHO's 2023 guidance.

    This is the most frequent single cause of elimination in this vertical, roughly 52% of all sweetener rejections, so the caveat matters. The sucralose-6-acetate paper is an in vitro screening study, it is contested by industry and by several toxicologists, and no regulator has changed sucralose's status because of it. That is why you will see us put it as "a 2023 in vitro study reported…" and why you will not see us describe sucralose as genotoxic.

  • Allura Red AC

    One of the six colours that must carry the EU warning "may have an adverse effect on activity and attention in children". A dye that comes with a child-attention warning has no place in a family catalogue, and colour does nothing for you anyway.

    The EU response to the Southampton study was a mandatory warning label, not a ban. We rest the rejection on the warning label, which is a regulatory fact, not on a settled causal finding about any one child.
