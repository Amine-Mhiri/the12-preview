The shelves

Each type below holds twelve slots at most. Where you see fewer, nothing else we analysed cleared the filter — an empty slot is a result, not a gap.

Protein

Grams that count, from sources you can name.

The largest category in sport nutrition and the one with the most creative labelling. We read the amino profile before the marketing, reject anything spiked to inflate a protein reading, and rank on protein per calorie, leucine per serving and what the rest of the tub is made of.

  • Whey Protein Isolate12/12The highest protein-per-calorie option, with the lactose largely filtered out.19 analysed · 7 rejected
  • Whey Protein Concentrate6/12Less processed than isolate, more of the native whey fractions, lower price per gram.18 analysed · 10 rejected
  • Casein & Slow-Release Protein7/12Slow-digesting protein for the long gap between meals, usually taken at night.17 analysed · 9 rejected
  • Plant Protein10/12Dairy-free protein that still delivers a complete amino profile.24 analysed · 13 rejected
  • Collagen Peptides7/12Connective-tissue support. Not a muscle protein — we grade it as what it is.21 analysed · 14 rejected
  • Protein Bars5/12Protein you can keep in a bag. The category where the worst labels hide.20 analysed · 7 rejected
  • Ready-to-Drink Protein9/12Shelf-stable liquid protein for when a shaker is not an option.18 analysed · 6 rejected

Performance

Compounds with trials behind them, at the doses used in those trials.

Creatine, caffeine, citrulline and beta-alanine have decades of evidence. Almost everything else sold alongside them does not. This category is graded hardest on dose: a clinically studied ingredient at a decorative amount scores zero on efficacy regardless of how clean the label is.

  • Creatine Monohydrate12/12The most studied performance supplement in existence. Also the easiest to overpay for.16 analysed · 2 rejected
  • Pre-Workout4/12Stimulant and pump formulas. The category with the highest rejection rate we have.16 analysed · 12 rejected
  • EAA & BCAA9/12Free-form amino acids for training on an empty stomach.15 analysed · 6 rejected
  • Beta-Alanine10/12Buffers the burn in efforts lasting one to four minutes.12 analysed · 1 rejected
  • Citrulline & Nitric Oxide9/12Blood-flow support taken before training, without the stimulants.12 analysed · 2 rejected

Hydration

Electrolytes for a climate that takes them seriously.

Training in Kuwait means sweating at rates that a temperate-climate label was never designed for. We weight sodium content higher here than any other market would, and we reject the flavoured-water products that sell on packaging alone.

  • Electrolytes8/12Sodium, potassium and magnesium replacement — which in Kuwait is not optional.16 analysed · 7 rejected
  • Intra-Workout Carbohydrate6/12Fast fuel for sessions past the ninety-minute mark.12 analysed · 5 rejected

Daily Essentials

The unglamorous four that actually change blood work.

Omega-3, vitamin D3, magnesium and a sensible multivitamin. No category is more affected by ingredient *form*: magnesium oxide and magnesium glycinate are not the same purchase, and most labels are betting you don't know that.

  • Omega-39/12EPA and DHA. The supplement most people take at a dose too small to matter.18 analysed · 9 rejected
  • Vitamin D3 & K29/12The one deficiency that is near-universal in a climate where nobody trains outdoors at noon.18 analysed · 9 rejected
  • Magnesium10/12Sleep, cramp and recovery support. Form matters more here than in any other category.18 analysed · 8 rejected
  • Athlete Multivitamin9/12Insurance against the gaps a training diet leaves. Not a performance product.18 analysed · 9 rejected
  • Zinc10/12Immune and hormonal support, at a dose that does not displace copper.18 analysed · 8 rejected

Recovery

What happens between sessions decides the next one.

Sleep and joint support, filtered for Kuwait's import rules as well as our own doctrine. Where a compound cannot lawfully reach Kuwait, we leave the slot empty rather than list something you cannot receive.

  • Sleep & Recovery9/12Getting to sleep after an evening session, without a compound that leaves you flat.17 analysed · 8 rejected
  • Joint Support9/12Connective tissue under load. A category thick with underdosed formulas.18 analysed · 9 rejected

Body Composition

Calories, engineered honestly.

Mass gainers and meal replacements are judged as food rather than as supplements, which is why most of the category fails: a product built on maltodextrin and sucralose is not a meal, whatever the tub says.

  • Mass Gainer3/12Calories in a shaker, for people who genuinely cannot eat enough.18 analysed · 11 rejected
  • Meal Replacement3/12A complete meal when there is no meal. Judged as food, not as a supplement.17 analysed · 9 rejected
