Why this one did not make the twelve

It broke 6 of our rules. A hard rule is binary: there is no score to argue about and no weighting that could have saved it.

  • Allura Red AC

    A3.01

    One of the six colours that must carry the EU warning "may have an adverse effect on activity and attention in children". A dye that comes with a child-attention warning has no place in a family catalogue, and colour does nothing for you anyway.

    Where we have to be carefulThe EU response to the Southampton study was a mandatory warning label, not a ban. We rest the rejection on the warning label, which is a regulatory fact, not on a settled causal finding about any one child.

    • Reg. (EC) 1333/2008 on food additives (Art. 24 + Annex V)
    • McCann et al., Lancet 370(9598):1560–1567 (2007) — the Southampton study
    • EFSA evaluation of the Southampton study
    • HHS/FDA announcement on phasing out petroleum-based synthetic dyes, 22 Apr 2025

  • Sunset Yellow FCF

    A3.03

    One of the six colours that must carry the EU child-attention warning. There is no reason for a supplement to be orange.

    Where we have to be carefulTo be accurate about this one: EFSA lowered the ADI to 1 mg/kg bw/day on a temporary basis in 2009, but restored it to 4 mg/kg bw/day in its 2014 reconsideration. Our rejection rests on the Annex V warning label, not on the ADI.

    • Reg. (EC) 1333/2008 on food additives (Art. 24 + Annex V)
    • McCann et al., Lancet 370(9598):1560–1567 (2007) — the Southampton study
    • EFSA evaluation of the Southampton study
    • EFSA 2014 reconsideration of the Sunset Yellow ADI

  • Brilliant Blue FCF

    A3.08

    A petroleum-derived synthetic dye with no functional benefit to you. Named in the FDA and HHS announcement of April 2025 asking industry to eliminate six remaining synthetic dyes by the end of 2026.

    Where we have to be carefulWorth knowing: the April 2025 FDA/HHS announcement is a stated intent and a request, not a rule, and no binding rulemaking accompanied it. Brilliant Blue is also not one of the Southampton Six colours. We exclude it under our own no-benefit class rule.

    • HHS/FDA announcement on phasing out petroleum-based synthetic dyes, 22 Apr 2025
    • 21 CFR Part 74 — listing of colour additives
    • California School Food Safety Act (AB 2316, signed 28 Sep 2024)

  • Titanium dioxide

    A3.13

    Banned as a food additive in the EU since 2022, after EFSA concluded it could no longer be considered safe because a concern for genotoxicity could not be ruled out and no safe intake level could be established. It is there to make things white.

    Where we have to be carefulIn this category E171 usually hides in the capsule shell or the tablet coating rather than the main panel, so that is where we look for it. One point worth being precise about: California's Prop 65 lists only airborne, unbound titanium dioxide particles of respirable size. The food additive is not on that list, and we would not want you to come away from this thinking it is.

    • EFSA 2021 opinion on titanium dioxide (PMC8101360)
    • EFSA news release — E171 no longer considered safe
    • Reg. (EU) 2022/63 — in force 7 Feb 2022

  • Proprietary blend with a single total weight

    A6.01

    The label gives one total weight for a group of ingredients instead of an amount for each. A 5,000 mg blend could be almost all of the cheapest thing in it. If a brand will not tell you how much is in it, we cannot tell you whether it works.

    Where we have to be carefulProprietary blends are entirely legal under 21 CFR 101.36(c). This is a disqualification on our terms, not an accusation of law-breaking: a product whose dose cannot be computed cannot be ranked, and a platform that ranks unrankable things is guessing. Both NSF Certified for Sport and Informed Sport already operate on full quantitative disclosure, so we are asking for what the best brands already do.

    • 21 CFR 101.36(c) — proprietary blend labelling
    • the12 curation methodology §A6

  • Active named with no individual amount

    A6.02

    An active ingredient is named on the panel or in the marketing with no amount next to it. Descending order of predominance tells you nothing about magnitude.

    Where we have to be carefulWhere the amounts are simply omitted rather than hidden inside a declared blend, the elimination is recorded as non-adjudicable (A0.04, R12) instead.

    • 21 CFR 101.36(c) — proprietary blend labelling
