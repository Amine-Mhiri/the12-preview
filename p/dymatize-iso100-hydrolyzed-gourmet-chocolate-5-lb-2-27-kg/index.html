Why this one did not make the twelve

It broke 2 of our rules. A hard rule is binary: there is no score to argue about and no weighting that could have saved it.

  • Acesulfame potassium

    A2.02

    Safe by the regulator's assessment. We still exclude it, under WHO's 2023 guidance on non-sugar sweeteners and because it is the co-sweetener most often stacked with sucralose in this category.

    Where we have to be carefulTo be accurate about this one: EFSA completed a full re-evaluation in 2025 and raised the acceptable daily intake to 15 mg/kg of body weight per day, finding European exposure below that level in every population group. We still exclude it, but that is a values position rather than a toxicological finding, and we would rather say so than imply the regulators are on our side.

    • WHO guideline: use of non-sugar sweeteners (2023)
    • EFSA 2025 re-evaluation of acesulfame K (PMC12041894)

  • Sucralose

    A2.04

    A 2023 in vitro study raised questions about a manufacturing impurity of sucralose. The regulators have not moved. We have — and we exclude it, as we do the other synthetic high-intensity sweeteners, under WHO's 2023 guidance.

    Where we have to be carefulThis is the most frequent single cause of elimination in this vertical, roughly 52% of all sweetener rejections, so the caveat matters. The sucralose-6-acetate paper is an in vitro screening study, it is contested by industry and by several toxicologists, and no regulator has changed sucralose's status because of it. That is why you will see us put it as "a 2023 in vitro study reported…" and why you will not see us describe sucralose as genotoxic.

    • WHO guideline: use of non-sugar sweeteners (2023)
    • Schiffman et al. 2023, J Toxicol Environ Health B 26(6):307–341
